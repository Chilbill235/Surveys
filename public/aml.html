AML and KYC Policy

Last updated: September 30, 2026

This policy explains how RewardZone ("we") identifies account holders, screens activity for money laundering and terrorist financing, and handles an account when something does not add up. It is published separately from our Terms of Service so that the compliance position is readable on its own, and it should be read with our Privacy Policy.

1. What AML and KYC mean here

AML is anti-money-laundering: the rules and the work that stop money being moved through a service in order to disguise where it came from. KYC is know-your-customer: identifying who an account belongs to before it is used. AML is the screening; KYC is the identification. We do both, in that order, and neither is optional.

A platform that pays out real money is a plausible route for that money, which is why we are explicit about this rather than leaving it as a paragraph in the Terms.

2. Who we identify

To hold a balance and receive a withdrawal you must be able to confirm an email address and, in the case of a withdrawal, complete a verification step against the address on the account. We record the email address you signed up with, the identity of the account, the payment instruments you have used, and the transactions you have made.

We do not ask for a government identity document, a date of birth, or a proof of address to simply hold a balance and complete offers. We ask for the minimum that lets you withdraw, and we ask for it at the point of withdrawal rather than at sign-up, so that joining costs nothing.

3. How we screen

Screening happens at the points where value moves: when funds are deposited, when a withdrawal is requested, and when an account's activity is reviewed. We look for patterns that are inconsistent with normal use of the Platform rather than trying to reach a conclusion about any single transaction.

  • Deposit sources that do not match the account holder's expected activity.
  • Repeated deposits immediately followed by withdrawals, particularly to third-party destinations.
  • Attempts to pay a withdrawal to an address belonging to a different person.
  • Multiple accounts presenting similar details, or one account presenting several.
  • Activity from a destination we are asked to block, or from a restricted jurisdiction.

We also review reports of suspicious activity. Those are confidential, and we do not tell you that we have made one about you — that is a legal requirement, not an oversight, and no amount of support will change it.

4. What we do when something does not match

Most questions are answered quickly and without any interruption to your account. In escalating order, and in roughly the order they happen:

  • We ask. A short question, usually answered in the same email, and usually enough to clear it.
  • We hold the withdrawal. The funds stay credited to your balance and are not released until the question is answered. Your balance is not reduced and nothing is forfeited.
  • We restrict the account. Deposits and withdrawals stop. Offers and balance remain visible to you throughout.
  • We close the account and return any balance we are permitted to return, following the route you gave us.

We will always tell you which of these is happening. If a withdrawal is on hold, you will be told it is on hold and why, rather than being left to wonder why the money has stopped moving. Holding a withdrawal never means you have been accused of anything.

5. Restricted jurisdictions

We do not accept accounts from, or pay withdrawals to, people resident in jurisdictions we are required to exclude, or where operating would be unlawful. Joining from one of these places means you cannot be paid, so we would rather say so than take your details and then decline later. The current list is available from Contact support.

6. What we do not do

We do not profile you for advertising, we do not sell your data, and we do not use your identity for any purpose beyond running your account and meeting our legal obligations. The screening above is the only reason we collect what this policy describes.

7. Records and your rights

We keep the records these obligations require. That is why some data outlives the account it belongs to, and it is the one place where a deletion request does not take effect immediately — the rest of how we handle your data is set out in our Privacy Policy, and you can export everything the Platform holds about you as a spreadsheet from your account settings at any time.

8. Contact and complaints

Questions about this policy go through Contact support. If you are not satisfied with how a compliance decision was made, ask for it to be reviewed in the first place, and then escalate. The review will be carried out by someone who was not involved in the original decision.
